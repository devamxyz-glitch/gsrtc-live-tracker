Privacy policy

Last updated 25 August 2026

ST Tracker has no accounts, no adverts, and never sells anything to anyone. It does record how the app is used, and it links those records to a random ID stored on your device — so it can tell that the same install came back, without ever knowing who you are. This page says exactly what is kept, what is not, and how to erase it.

What stays on your device and never leaves it

These are stored in your browser only. They are not sent anywhere, not backed up to any server, and they disappear if you clear the app's data from Settings.

  • Your saved commutes, saved buses and recently viewed buses
  • Your chosen stop for arrival alerts
  • Your language, theme, text size, map style and refresh settings
  • Your last known location, kept so the "distance from you" figure can appear immediately

What is sent, and why

Bus data you ask for

When you track a bus or search a timetable, the app asks the operator's system for that information through this server. The request contains the bus number or the two stations — it does not contain anything about you. These requests are logged for a short period so that faults can be diagnosed, and those logs include an IP address, as with any web server.

Your location

Your location is used on your device to work out how far a bus is from you and to sort nearby stops. It is sent off the device in exactly one case: asking the operator which stations are near you, which cannot be answered locally. It is never stored on this server, and it is never attached to anything else.

How you use the app

The app records which features you use, and links those records together with a random ID created when you first open it. That ID is generated by your own device, is not derived from your hardware, your network or anything about you, and exists only so that separate visits can be recognised as the same install.

This is pseudonymous, not anonymous, and the difference matters enough to say plainly: individual visits can be seen as a sequence, and one install can be told apart from another. What cannot be done is connect any of it to a person — there is no account, no email, no phone number and no name anywhere in the app to connect it to.

It is used to decide what to build and what to fix — which features earn their place, where people give up, whether the Gujarati translation is worth maintaining. It is not used to target you with anything, and it is never shared or sold.

What is never recorded, even now

  • Your location, ever. Not once, and not as a trail. Where somebody is over time identifies them even with no name attached — a home and a workplace are enough — so the database has no column to put it in.
  • Anything about where you physically are. Your location is used on your device and is never stored on the server, in any form. There is no column for it.
  • Free text of any kind. Station searches, and anything else you type, are never recorded. The single exception is a bus number, which is listed in the table above — and it is checked to be a valid number plate before it is stored, so it cannot carry a message.
  • Your name, email, phone number, contacts, photos or files.
  • Advertising IDs, cross-site trackers, or anything given to a third party.

What that means in practice

Recording which buses you look up, against an ID that persists between visits, means the records could show that one install repeatedly checks a particular bus at a particular time of day. If you ride the same service every morning, that pattern is visible in the data — not tied to your name, because there isn't one, but visible.

It is kept because it is what makes the app better at the thing it exists for: knowing which services people actually depend on decides which get accurate timetables, crowding predictions and alerts first. It is never sold, never shared, and never used to contact or target anyone.

If you would rather it were not recorded, the switch below turns it off, and the reset deletes everything already held for your install. Neither changes how the app works.

Erasing it

Settings → Reset my analytics ID deletes everything ever recorded for this install from the server, and issues a new ID, so nothing afterwards joins up with anything before. It is a real deletion, not a flag.

Settings → Help improve the app → Off stops the recording entirely. Your device sends nothing; it does not send it and ask the server to ignore it.

Records older than 90 days are deleted automatically.

Reports about buses

If you report how full a bus is, that it never came, or that a different bus ran the trip, that report is stored so other riders can see it. It records the bus, what you said, and the day — not who you are. To stop the same person reporting the same bus repeatedly, a scrambled, shortened one-way version of your IP address is kept alongside it; it cannot be turned back into an address, and it is not used for anything else. Reports are deleted after 30 days.

Arrival alerts

If you ask to be notified when a bus is near, your browser gives the app an anonymous notification address from Google or Apple. That address, the bus, and the point you want to be told about are stored so the alert can be sent. There is no name or account attached. The alert is deleted once it fires or expires, and you can cancel it at any time — after which nothing about it remains.

Others involved

  • GSRTC's tracking provider answers the requests for bus positions and timetables. Those requests carry the bus or stations you asked about.
  • Map tiles are fetched from CARTO and Esri. Loading an image from them reveals your IP address and the map area, as any image on the web does. No account or identifier is attached.
  • Google or Apple deliver push notifications if you enable arrival alerts.

ST Tracker is an independent community app. It is not operated by, affiliated with, or endorsed by GSRTC.

Children

The app is a public transport timetable. It is not directed at children and collects nothing that would identify anyone of any age.

Removing your data

Settings → Clear saved data removes everything held on your device. Cancelling an arrival alert removes it from the server. Usage counts and bus reports contain nothing that identifies you, so there is nothing personal to retrieve or erase — but if you have a question, the contact below is a real person who will answer.

Changes

If what is collected ever changes, the date at the top of this page changes with it. The app will not begin collecting something that identifies you without saying so plainly first.
